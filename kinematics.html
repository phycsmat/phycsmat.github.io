The study of motion without worrying about what causes it.

What is Kinematics?

Kinematics is a branch of physics that describes how objects move. It focuses on quantities such as position, displacement, velocity, speed, and acceleration without discussing the forces causing the motion.

Important Quantities

1. Distance

Distance is the total path traveled by an object. It does not care about direction. It is a scalar quantity.

2. Displacement

Displacement is the change in position from the starting point to the ending point. It includes direction, so it is a vector quantity.

3. Speed

Speed tells us how fast an object moves. It is the rate of change of distance.

\[ v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \]

4. Velocity

Velocity is similar to speed, but it includes direction. It is the rate of change of displacement.

Velocity = Displacement / Time

5. Acceleration

Acceleration tells us how quickly velocity changes with time. An object accelerates when it speeds up, slows down, or changes direction.

Acceleration = Change in Velocity / Time

Example

Suppose a car moves 100 meters in 5 seconds in a straight line. Its average speed is:

Speed = 100 / 5 = 20 m/s

If the car moves in a straight line in one direction, then its average velocity is also 20 m/s.

Why Kinematics Matters

Kinematics is the foundation of classical mechanics. Before studying force and energy, students need to understand how motion is described. It is also useful in engineering, astronomy, sports analysis, and many real-world systems.
